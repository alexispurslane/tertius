# The layer as the slab: the pyramid's build reads the back share, and the 10 % rule leaves the walk

Step 4 of the design `docs/plans/back-face-depth-layer.md`. Read that document first: its terms, "The model", decisions 3, 6 and 7, and "The interfaces". This ticket changes the image: the real back face becomes the slab of every pixel.

## System design

After ticket `t1-0s0a`, the pyramid's build computes each back w by the 10 % rule. After ticket `t1-j03y`, the back share of each pixel exists. This ticket joins them: the build computes each back w from the back share. The walk does not change.

| State | Writer | Before | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| The back w of a pixel at the finest level | `depth_pyramid_finest.comp` | `centerW / 1.1` | `min(slabBack(centerW, share), planeFarW)` |
| The back w of a pixel's term at level 1 | `depth_pyramid_first.comp` | `planeFarW / 1.1` | `slabBack(planeFarW, share)` |
| The levels above | The down and tail shaders | The merge | Unchanged |
| The back share | `BackFacePass` | Read by nothing | Read by the two shaders above, and by nothing else |

What causes what: the G-buffer pass draws the front surface. The back-face pass then writes the back share. The pyramid's build then turns front and share into the slab. The walk then reads one texel per step, as today. So the frame's order is G-buffer, back faces, depth pyramid, tracer.

The slab rule lives in one function, `slabBack`, and only the pyramid's build calls it. The walk and the tracer hold no thickness.

The invariant that the walk depends on stays: the slab of every cell holds the slab of every pixel under it. The reason is in the design's decision 3.

## Signatures

GLSL, in `depth_pyramid.glsl`:

```glsl
// The w of the back of what is solid behind a surface at inverse depth `w` whose back share is `share`: `w * share`
// for a share between 0 and 1, and `w` itself for a sheet, whose share is 0 or 1.
float slabBack(float w, float share);

// The slab of a pixel at the finest level: x the center w, y the back w, or (0, 0) for sky.
vec2 pixelSlab(ivec2 pixel);

// The bounds of a pixel for level 1: x its plane's near w, y the back w of its slab from its plane's far w, or the
// empty bounds for sky.
vec2 pixelSlabBounds(ivec2 pixel);
```

The two build shaders read the back share through a binding of the depth pyramid's own sets. Binding 0 of its finest set and of its level-1 set is unwritten today, and the probe used it. No descriptor layout of the tracer changes.

`thickness.glsl` and `CellBounds.ThicknessShare` leave the tree. Their two other readers get constants of their own:

```glsl
// In screen_trace.glsl: the depth of the box that a guided diffuse ray aims at, as a share of the target's depth.
const float TargetDepthShare = 0.05;

// In screen_trace_plan.comp: how far a neighbor's point can lie off a pixel's plane and still fit it, as a share of
// the neighbor's view depth.
const float PlaneFitShare = 0.1;
```

The C# copies follow: `DepthPyramid` builds its levels from a front distance and a back share, and `LightPyramid.TargetDepthShare` holds 0.05 itself. `ScreenRay.Walk` does not change in this ticket. Ticket `t1-0s0a` delivered `CellBounds(Near, Back)`, `CellBounds.SlabBack`, `PixelSurface.SlabBounds`, `DepthPyramid.PixelSlab` and `DepthPyramid.WalkSlab`. The slab rule of this ticket goes into `SlabBack` and its two callers.

## Settled decisions

- The light of a blocked ray stays `edgeFade(hit) * (1.0 - entryShare)`, and the entry share is measured over the real slab. No line of `traceFrom` changes.
- A sheet's slab reaches to its plane's far w. The parent agent settled it on 2026-11-15: it states the far end of the sheet's real extent inside its pixel, and it is no thickness.
- The slab that also starts at the plane's near w is not built here. It moves the front w that other readers share. Ticket `t1-0ti7` plans that rule.
- The probe's mode `layer/plane` is this rule: `docs/research/back-face-depth-layer/stage-1b/probe-slab-in-pyramid.patch` and `stage-2/probe-cards.patch`. They are starting points, not the code.
- Each reference image that changes gets a real review, as CLAUDE.md, "Testing", says: the reference, the actual image and the FLIP heat map. The report describes each one. A scene that becomes darker because false bounce light left is the expected change.
