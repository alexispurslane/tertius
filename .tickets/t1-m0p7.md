# The 8-bit output has no dither: a smooth, dark wall shows contour steps, and the daub measure counts them as blobs

Found by the surface share's lead on 2026-11-23 in the guide census (t1-nyjl), with the bay's diffuse signal shown alone.

## What happens

The frozen bay's back wall below the slot is a smooth, dark gradient. Its blob band (the daub's band, 3 to 36 px, of the still's relative deviation from the late mean) correlates 0.93 to 0.95 with itself at every lag from 33 to 396 draws, at a history cap of 32 and of 128. Ray noise through a history of 32 keeps 0.35 after 33 draws and nothing after 99. The band correlates 0.965 with the band of the reference itself rounded to 8 bits, at the same size (standard deviation 0.0447 against 0.0431 in the lead's units). So the largest blob of that region is the 8-bit rounding of the shown image, not the rays. On the bay's right wall the same steps show as soft slanted bands in the diffuse picture; they are not in the reference, and in a single capture the noise makes them wander (right-wall-bands.png, each panel stretched 12 times around its own 36 px blur). Evidence: lifetime.json, quant.json, right-wall-bands.png, lifetime.py, quant.py, bands.py in t3-2b720d96-b40221aeff8f27f7-evidence/guide/.

## Why it is the game's fault and not only the capture's

Checked in the code by the line orchestrator: no shader of the engine dithers (the only match for the word is in FSR's own header), and SwapchainTarget prefers the 8-bit sRGB formats (B8G8R8A8Srgb, R8G8B8A8Srgb, then the 8-bit UNORM ones). HeadlessTarget is R8G8B8A8_SRGB. So a window shows the same 8-bit steps as a capture. In the darks one sRGB code is a step of several percent in light, which a smooth wall shows as contour lines. Not checked: how visible the steps are on the user's display at the game's exposure. The lead's picture is stretched 12 times.

## The second damage: the measure

The daub and the blob band read 8-bit PNG captures. On a smooth, dark region they measure the rounding. The back wall's daub of 0.0132 (bar about 0.010) did not move with the guided share (0.0096 to 0.0100 in the blob band) or with the history cap (to 0.0078), because most of it is not ray noise. In the other three regions of the bay the rounding's band is 10 to 25 times smaller than the blob band. Until the output dithers, a daub on a dark smooth region needs the rounding's band beside it (quant.py gives it), or a capture before the rounding.

## Direction, for whoever takes it

Add a dither of one code's width at the last write to an 8-bit target, in the encoded domain, from the blue-noise mask that the tracer already holds. With an sRGB format the hardware applies the curve after the shader's write, so the dither must be sized in the encoded value: either scale it by the curve's slope, or write to the UNORM format and encode in the shader, a path that the swapchain's fallback already has. A 10-bit format where the surface offers one (A2B10G10R10 is already known to SwapchainTarget) lowers the step four times and does not remove it. The dither must not feed the upscaler's or the tracer's histories: it goes after them. A test: a smooth dark ramp through the output has no run of equal codes longer than the dither allows, and its mean over a region equals the unrounded mean.

## Design (the line orchestrator of the Design X line, 2026-11-23, 14:11 New York time)

The orchestrator gave this ticket to the Design X line and settled its main points. A lead reverses none of them without a measured reason. The points that I added are marked as mine.

### Terms

- **A code** is one step of the output target's stored value: 1 / 255 of the encoded range for an 8-bit format.
- **The encoded value** is the color after the sRGB curve, from 0 to 1, as the target stores it. The light before the curve is the linear value.
- **The dither** is a small offset in the encoded value, added before the target rounds to a code, so that the mean over a region equals the unrounded value.

### The model

The picture is rounded to codes once, at the last write of the picture to the output target. That write is the tone map pass (ToneMapPass, tonemap.glsl). The frame's order is Upscale, ToneMap, Overlay, Readback (FrameGraph.Stage), so the tone map runs after the upscaler and after every history, and no history reads its result. The window (SwapchainTarget) and the headless target (HeadlessTarget) both get their picture from this one pass, so a capture shows what the window shows. The dither goes into this pass and nowhere else.

The dither is a pure function of the pixel, the frame's draw index and the target's code. It keeps no state. Its noise comes from the blue-noise mask that the frame set already holds (BlueNoiseMask, uploaded by BrdfTableImages; screen_trace.glsl reads it as blueNoiseMask), at the mask's frame for the draw index.

### Settled by the orchestrator

1. One dither at the last write to an 8-bit target, in the encoded domain, one code wide, triangular, from the blue-noise mask with the frame's number. A run repeats on the same machine.
2. One choke point for the window and the headless target.
3. Written to the Vulkan spec, with no reliance on what MoltenVK does at an sRGB write. If the dither is sized by the curve's slope for an sRGB format, a test pins it on lavapipe too.
4. A 10-bit format is not this ticket.

### The signature

    // The encoded color with the output's dither: a triangular offset of at most one code up or down, the same for the
    // three channels, from the blue-noise mask at the pixel and the draw. It is 0 at an encoded value of 0 and of 1.
    vec3 dithered(vec3 encoded, ivec2 pixel, uint drawIndex, float code)

    // In main, after the tone curve:
    vec3 encoded = dithered(linearToSrgb(ldr), ivec2(gl_FragCoord.xy), tonemap.drawIndex, tonemap.code);
    outColor = vec4(tonemap.encodeSrgb != 0u ? encoded : srgbToLinear(encoded), 1.0);

- For a UNORM target the shader writes the encoded value, as today's encodeSrgb path does.
- For an sRGB target the shader takes the encoded value back through the inverse curve, and the hardware's curve then gives the dithered encoded value again. This form needs no slope, so it does not depend on how a driver rounds. (My choice between the ticket's two forms. If the round trip costs precision that a test shows, the slope form is the fallback.)
- tonemap.code is the target's code, 1 / 255 for the 8-bit formats, set by ToneMapPass from the output's format in one C# function. tonemap.drawIndex is the frame's draw index.

### Points that I added

- **The ends of the range.** The dither's size falls to 0 at the encoded values 0 and 1, over one code. So a black pixel stays code 0 and a white pixel stays code 255, and a black frame holds no noise.
- **One value for the three channels.** A grey wall then stays grey, and the dither adds no color speckle.
- **A switch for tests.** RendererOptions gets an output dither setting, on by default for the window and for headless runs. A test that asserts exact codes of a flat color turns it off and says why. A snapshot test keeps it on, because the user sees it.
- **The mask's binding.** The tone map pass uses the post layout and not the frame set. The lead chooses how the pass reads the mask (the frame set beside the post set, or one more binding of the post set) and says which in the report. The mask keeps one owner.

### Acceptance

1. The red test, on today's code: a smooth dark ramp through the output has no run of equal codes longer than the dither allows, and its mean over a region equals the unrounded mean. It runs for an sRGB target and for a UNORM target, and on lavapipe.
2. Tests: black stays code 0 and white stays code 255; the same pixel and draw give the same code twice; two draws give different patterns.
3. Pictures before the gates, to the line orchestrator the moment they exist: the frozen bay's back wall and one dark cabin wall, main beside the dither, at 1:1 and stretched 12 times; and a film of 3 s of a slow pan over a dark gradient, main beside the dither, by the video rules, to show that the dither does not crawl or shimmer.
4. The back wall's daub with the rounding's band beside it (the back-face line's quant.py), before and after.
5. The gates: Checked, then Release; the mutants of the changed lines by tools/mutate.py; coverage; one review. ARCHITECTURE.md, "Tone mapping", says what the dither is and why it is there.

### Out of scope

A 10-bit output format. Any change of the daub's measure itself.

2026-11-23, line orchestrator: CORRECTION of this ticket's first two sections. The claim that the back wall's blob is the 8-bit rounding is withdrawn. The lead found the fault in its own tools: lifetime.py and quant.py took a relative residual (shown / reference - 1) over the whole image and then blurred it with the 36 px Gaussian, so near-black pixels outside a region, whose relative values are huge, leaked into the region. Both the blob band and the rounding band held the same leak, which gave the correlation of 0.965. Measured as an absolute residual (shown - reference), the rounding's band is 0.0006 to 0.0008 of the region's mean in all four regions of the bay, against blob bands of 0.0068 to 0.0294, and its correlation with the blob band is 0.000 to 0.001. The back wall's blobs live as a plain history of 32 does (0.32, 0.10, 0.04 at one, two and three captures). The daub, parts.py and blotch.py had no such fault. So the second section, the damage to the measure, is also withdrawn: the daub does not count the rounding to any size that matters, and every blob number of the bay is 10 to 50 times its rounding band.

What remains true: no shader of the engine dithers, the swapchain prefers the 8-bit sRGB formats, and the right wall's soft slanted bands in the diffuse picture are 8-bit contour steps (bands.py crops before it divides, so it has no leak; right-wall-bands.png shows them stretched 12 times). On the back wall, at sRGB codes 105 to 133, one code is a step of about 1.9 % in light and the rounding's error is 0.44 %. Whether such steps are visible unstretched on a smooth dark wall is not shown by any picture so far. So this ticket is a small one: a possible banding of a smooth dark gradient, with no evidence yet that the eye sees it. Whoever takes it first makes an unstretched 1:1 crop of a smooth dark gradient and looks, before any build. The old tables are kept as lifetime-rel.json; the corrected ones are lifetime-abs-*.json and quant-abs.json in t3-2b720d96-b40221aeff8f27f7-evidence/guide/.
