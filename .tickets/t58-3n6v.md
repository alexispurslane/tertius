# The catch-up kind of the render scale gets about 8 measurements per reset, so a cheaper scene or a first-draw outlier keeps its catch-up draws small for many resets

Found in the review of ter-bcwr. RenderScaleController keeps one fitted cost model and one request per cost kind of a draw. The catch-up kind is measured only in the 8 draws after each reset, but its rates are the ordinary kind's, which were tuned for a measurement every draw: a rise moves a twentieth of the gap, a fall a quarter, and the first measurement of a kind becomes its whole model with no smoothing.

- A reset in a cheaper view than the last one: one reset closes about 1 - 0.95^8 = 34 % of the gap, so the request is within 0.02 of the new scale only after about 6 resets (docs/research/ter-bcwr-announced-dip, 'A reset in a scene of another cost': fit at 40 ms per unit of area, reset in 20, the request goes 0.556 to 0.573 against a wanted 0.775).
- An outlier in the first catch-up draws (static reasoning, not run): on a first launch on a cold card, draw 0 costs about 230 ms (t58-phgr). That measurement becomes the whole catch-up model, which still holds about 63 ms per unit of area after draws 1 to 7, so the catch-up request ends the start at about 0.55, and each later cut draws its catch-up draws at about 0.55 to 0.65 in place of about 0.77, climbing about 0.03 per reset.

The rates and the band belong to ter-5kz6, which another session holds; this ticket records the catch-up kind's own need for them: for example a rise rate per kind, or a smoothed first fit. Build nothing before ter-5kz6 settles the rates.

- The fault shows. The catch-up request at the first catch-up draw of each cut, cold against warm: 0.538 against 0.770, 0.571 against 0.787, 0.653 against 0.805. The cold run's catch-up draws take 10.4 to 15.0 ms against a budget of 15 ms, so they are drawn smaller than needed. The gap closes by a factor of about 0.69 per cut; within 0.02 at about the 8th cut (a fit over two cuts, not a measurement).
- The cause of draw 0's cost is not a compile inside the draw. Draw 0 costs 229.0 ms cold and 29.4 ms warm, and every pass is 8 to 30 times dearer (diffuse rays 60.2 against 5.8 ms, direct light 21.4 against 2.1 ms). The pipelines compile on the CPU before draw 0 (wall time 56 s against 16 s). Before the cold run the card stood at its idle clock (210 MHz, state P8), after it at 2475 MHz (P0). The clocks' ratio of 11.8 fits the passes' ratio. Draw 1 is normal: 24.7 ms cold, 26.0 ms warm. So draw 0 is a draw that the GPU began from rest.
- Why one draw lasts for cuts: the first measurement of a kind becomes its whole model, each later measurement moves the model by 0.2, and the catch-up kind gets 8 measurements per reset. After the start's 8 draws the model still holds 0.8^7 = 21 % of draw 0. The request falls a quarter of its gap per measurement and rises a twentieth.
- Apart from draw 0: the cold run's ordinary request rests at 0.943 against the warm run's 0.992 for the whole run, because the cold process draws more slowly (FSR 1.76 against 1.40 ms at one output size). That is t1-80jd, a true cost that the controller follows. It is not part of this fix.

**The model.** A measurement means: the cost of this scene at this render area at the GPU's working speed. A draw that the GPU began from rest is not such a measurement, whatever its kind.

| Point | Design A: the run's first draw is no measurement | Design B: a kind's first fit is the median of its first three measurements |
|---|---|---|
| What it knows | The cause: the GPU starts the run's first draw from rest | Nothing of the cause: at most one outlier among a kind's first three measurements |
| What it covers | The measured fault, in every kind | One outlier in the first three of each kind, in any run. Not a wake-up of two draws |
| What it costs | One draw without a measurement. Draw 1 is the first fit, and it is normal on the card | A kind's first request comes two measurements later. At a start the catch-up draws stay at the ordinary scale for about 4 of the 8 draws, so they miss the frame time longer |
| New state | One fact in the controller: whether its first recorded draw is past | Up to three pending measurements per kind |
| A chosen number | None | 3 |

Usage, design A: no call changes. `Measure` changes nothing for the first draw that `Drew` recorded in the controller's life, with the reason in its comment.
Usage, design B: no call changes. `Measure` collects a kind's first three measurements and fits their median.

Both share: the rates and the band stay as they are (ter-5kz6), a test first, and one more cold job on the card as the acceptance: after a cold start the catch-up request at the first cut is within 0.02 of the warm run's.

My recommendation: design A. It states the measured cause, adds no number, and feeds the path that exists ("a frame that was not recorded changes nothing"). What it leaves out, and I say so: a draw from rest later in a run, for example after the window was hidden for a time. No take shows that case yet. If it shows, the same rule extends to it: a draw after a pause of the GPU's work is no measurement.

What neither design changes: the catch-up request still follows a cheaper scene only after about 6 resets (the first part of this ticket). That stays with the rates of ter-5kz6.
