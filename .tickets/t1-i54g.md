# A sheet with no thickness keeps only 25 % to 56 % of the real hits that the 10 % rule finds on it: find the cause

Found in stage 1b (docs/research/back-face-depth-layer/stage-1b/pictures.md, part 3, 'Can a ray hit a slab of zero thickness'). A sheet is a surface pixel with no back face behind it. A real hit is a hit of the census's class 1: the ray crosses the hit pixel's plane ahead of its start, where the eye sees that surface within 2 %. On the sheets of lamp-hall-16-hair and skin-and-hair, the rule NoBackSheet (back w = front w) keeps 25 % to 56 % of the 10 % rule's real hits. The rule NoBackSheetPlane (the sheet is as deep as its own plane over the pixel's square) keeps 52 % to 89 %. The cause is not known. The stair case of t1-n8x9 is a weak candidate: 99.7 % of those sheets face the camera, with a change of the front distance below 0.3 % per pixel. Other candidates, not checked: the crossing falls in a cutout hole between strands and the 10 % slab of the next strand catches the ray, the jitter moves the pixel's sample, or the finest level's flat center w misses a crossing in the half of the pixel where the plane is nearer. Count, for the lost hits, where the ray crosses the card's plane against the pixel that the 10 % rule hits.

## Acceptance Criteria

The record names the cause of the lost hits with a count, and the design states a sheet rule that keeps the real hits or says why it accepts the loss.
