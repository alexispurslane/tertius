# Depth pyramid and reflections within their share of the 12 ms frame: 4.09 ms to 2.59 ms on demo-exit

Part 3 of docs/plans/rendering-performance.md, split out of ter-3g1g by the orchestrator on 2026-10-31, so that the largest groups of passes are cut in parallel instead of one after another. The measurement (ter-3g1g, note of 2026-10-31): demo-exit at 1920 x 1080, Release, main 87ee9caf, Apple M1 Max, load 2 to 3. Two runs without a trace gave a mean GPU span of 18.04 and 17.94 ms; a trace of every frame without counters gave 17.56 ms. Each pass's share is its own busy time per frame in that trace (the mean over all 3,889 frames), times (12 ms - 1.87 ms of gaps between passes) / 16.03 ms = 0.632.

The four passes of the reflections take, in ms per frame (own time, then share): depth pyramid 0.159 and 0.100, radiance pyramid 0.621 and 0.392, reflection trace 1.390 and 0.879, reflections 1.920 and 1.214. Together 4.090 ms against a share of 2.585 ms, 1.51 ms less. Part 2b (ter-ff6y, 2026-10-26) brought the radiance pyramid, the trace and the reflections to 3.27 ms in two traces; they now take 3.93 ms. ter-r5hb's 256 steps added about 0.14 ms; the rest of the growth is unexplained. The user decided on 2026-10-26 that the half-resolution trace stays the default.

## Acceptance Criteria

The four passes' own busy time on demo-exit (a trace of every frame, two runs or a paired trace) is at most 2.59 ms, or the changes that keep the image are exhausted and a note gives the remainder and each candidate default change with its measured saving and captures. A note explains the growth from 3.27 ms. Every change keeps the image by the pixel comparison of decision 7. ARCHITECTURE.md gives the new cost.

Portability: the list is a storage buffer and vkCmdDispatchIndirect reads its first 12 bytes, both core Vulkan 1.0 with no device feature; storage buffer atomics in a compute shader need no feature either (vertexPipelineStoresAndAtomics and fragmentStoresAndAtomics are for other stages). vkCmdUpdateBuffer clears the header (16 bytes, a multiple of 4, outside a render pass). The upsample counts at most 65,535 workgroups, the spec's least maxComputeWorkGroupCount[0], and each invocation strides over the list by 65,535 x 64 entries, so a longer list is still traced once; 1080p needs at most 32,400. Both dispatches have 64 invocations per workgroup (8 x 8 and 64 x 1). The list holds 16 + 4 W H bytes, 8.3 MB at 1080p; its descriptor's range stays within the spec's least maxStorageBufferRange (2^27 bytes) up to 33.5 million pixels, so 8K UHD (7680 x 4320) fits. A larger screen relies on the device's limit, as the screen targets already rely on maxImageDimension2D above the spec's least 4096.

Change 4, kept: the radiance pyramid's downsample shares its corner taps through shared memory (builder, fix stage, 2026-11-01). The review found that the profile note dismissed ter-ff6y's two radiance-pyramid builds under the old 2-level rule. So this stage rebuilt both under the refined rule. Where a level's source has an even size on both axes (levels 1 to 3 and 7 at 1080p), every one of the 13 taps of texel p lies on a source texel corner, from 2p - 1 to 2p + 3 on each axis. So each 8 x 8 workgroup first takes the bilinear tap at each of its 19 x 19 corners once, with the same sampler (linearClamp), into 5,776 B of shared memory, waits at a barrier, and then sums each texel's 13 corners with jimenez13. Before, up to four texels took each corner's tap. Levels with an odd source size keep the 13 taps per texel. Same method: the same taps of the same sampler at the same corners, with the same weights.
