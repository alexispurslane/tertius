# A spatial filter removes the diffuse specks that rare sunlit hits leave

User decision 2026-11-08: remove the dense diffuse specks with an SVGF-style spatial filter (Schied et al. 2017), not a clamp and not importance-sampled rays. Diagnosis in /private/tmp/tertius-cabin-specks-20261108/: in the orbit scene's cabin, sunlight through the windows lights a strip a few pixels wide on each window opening's side face, about 100 times as bright as the lamp-lit walls. One diffuse ray per 2x2 block per draw rarely hits it, so each hit leaves a speck. A hit reads the hit pixel's fast diffuse history, which copies the specks to the floor and walls. The diffuse slow history's cap of 32 frames stops the noise from going down after about 32 draws. The pixel history rule (1560c7305) made the specks about three times as frequent and sharp, because it removed an accidental bilinear blur. With no sun there are no specks. SVGF was made for real-time ray tracing at about one ray per pixel with a short history and a depth and normal buffer, which matches our renderer. Build: after the diffuse temporal step, a variance-guided a-trous wavelet filter with depth, normal and luminance edge stops, on the demodulated diffuse light if the signal allows it. The history feedback and the rays that hit read the filtered light, so specks stop spreading. A debug switch turns it off. It must keep the light: the image mean of a lit region stays within a few percent. It must not leak light across a depth edge or a crease. Measure its GPU time. Acceptance: the diagnosis's speck count (connected groups above twice their 7x7 median and 0.03 above it, per 10,000 pixels) falls far on the cabin's ceiling, floor and walls, on a running frame, not a fresh still. Show the user before and after screenshots of the cabin.

**2026-11-09T02:06:17Z**

Build (2026-11-08). Design, as built:
- What the diffuse signal holds: the demodulated light. The resolve weights each ray's light by cos θ / (π · density), and the composite multiplies the result by the surface's diffuse color. So the filter works on the demodulated light, and textures stay sharp.
- Variance: the temporal dispatch keeps the standard deviation of the samples' luminance in the alpha of the diffuse fast history (which held an unused 0 there). Exponential update with the fast weight w: (1 − w)(variance + w step²). Steps within 2^-9 of the fast luminance count as 0 (half-float rounding of the fast history's stall), so a noise-free sample keeps exactly 0. Histories younger than 4 frames take a 7 × 7 same-surface estimate times 4 / length, as the paper does.
- Five wavelet steps, 5 × 5 B-spline, taps 1 to 16 px apart. Stops: plane (linear to 0 at 1 % of the nearer view depth, the surface test's PlaneShare), geometric normal cosine^128, luminance exp(−Δl / (4 √(var_p + var_q))).
- Deviation from the paper: the paper's gather (each pixel divided by its own weight sum) made light, about 20 % on the cabin with the clamp off, because a quiet pixel took the light of noisy neighbors that lost almost nothing. The wavelet step moves light by a share w(p, q) that is the same in both directions and is not renormalized. The B-spline weights sum to 1 and each stop is at most 1, so the shares stay below 1; the weight that a stop removes stays with the pixel. On an even surface it is the paper's mean. A first version divided by max(W(p), W(q)), which needed a second dispatch per step (3–4 ms in all); the constant 1 is also conservative and costs half.
- Deviation from the paper: the luminance stop uses √(var_p + var_q) (the deviation of a difference of two independent luminances), so the weight is symmetric, which conservation needs. No g3x3 prefilter: each link already sees both pixels' variance. Tried √(2 min(var)) instead: worse loss and specks (native means −16/−12/−22 %, specks 9/4/4).
- Feedback: step 0's light goes into the slow history, as the paper feeds back its first step. Measured without feedback: worse loss. Hits read Filtered, the last step's output of the last frame: a hit's light enters another pixel's hemisphere integral, so a blur at q does not show but a speck does. Tried moving the fast history by the same light: it made a self-sustaining blur of a noise-free image; rejected.
- Debug switch TERTIUS_DIFFUSE_FILTER=off. The single-history prototype also turns the filter off.
- Formats: three RGBA16F images (mandatory storage). No extended format.
Left out, not needed now: a separate filter of the reflections; temporal variance of the slow history's window; per-step g3x3; shared-memory tiling of the small steps; a filter of the test signals' second part.
Acceptance (orbit cabin, person camera, 1280×720, FSR 2/3, draws 120–159 of a still camera):
- Specks per 10,000 px, off → on: ceiling 31.6 → 1.2, floor 17.1 → 0.5, left wall 26.2 → 0.1.
- Linear means at fixed exposure 4.08, bloom off, log tone map (no clipping), on against off: with FSR +3.1 / +0.2 / −3.2 %; without FSR −5.3 / −2.7 / −9.2 %. With the slow history's clamp off: −4.1 / +4.8 / −4.5 %. The native loss is the clamp cutting moved light at quiet pixels: td-hjn6 (linked to ter-18hc). Under auto exposure the shown means differ more because the exposure was 0.2 stops higher without the filter (the meter sees darker majority pixels when light sits in specks).
- GPU: frame span medians 11.73 / 11.71 ms on, 10.79 / 11.08 ms off (interleaved pairs, load ~6–10): about 0.6–0.9 ms. gpu-trace.sh could not match the timestamp encoders on this run ("No command buffer of Tertius.Demo holds 29 timestamp encoders"), so no trace time.
- Test finding: this GPU's half-float stores round toward zero (a 1/16 blend stops within 16 steps below the sample). Tests account for it.
