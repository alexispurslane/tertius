# Which bright strips does the sun really light: the jamb and the end window's sill of rect-lamps and the cabin's sill strips, and the cause of the leak in the sun's shadow

This ticket is an experiment, with no code for the branch. It is the diagnosis for bug ticket `t8-rjno`, and the parent agent made it the line's most urgent open ticket on 2026-11-15. Read first: `.tickets/t8-rjno.md`, `docs/research/back-face-depth-layer/stage-2/reference.md` (all of part 1 and part 3), and ARCHITECTURE.md, "Shadows", from "Shadow bias" to its known limits.

## Why it matters

The reference experiment found that the bright strip on a jamb of `rect-lamps` is direct sunlight. The sun's shadow lets it through, and a path trace shows no strip. Thin bright strips of sunlight are the named cause of several faults in two lines of work:

- The false sill hits of the screen walk in `rect-lamps` (`ter-ukw6`).
- The specks on the ceiling of `rect-lamps`.
- The 18-draw cycle of the jitter in the cabin's light.
- The rare bright samples that the history's box cuts.

If the strips are leaks, a fix at the source removes a cause that both lines now work around. So the first question is which strips the sun lights in the truth.

## The model of the diagnosis

A strip is sunlit in the truth where a straight line from its surface to the sun meets no shadow caster. That is a fact of geometry, and it does not depend on any renderer. The engine's sun shadow is an approximation of that fact: four cascades, a filter of 4 × 4 taps, a slope-scaled depth bias on the casters and a normal offset on the lookup. A leak is a place where the approximation says "lit" and the geometry says "blocked".

In both views the sun stands near the vertical: 0.26° from it in `rect-lamps`. So every vertical wall is a caster at an extreme slope to the light. ARCHITECTURE.md already names one limit of that kind: the slope-scaled bias moves a caster lit at a steep angle back by 2.5 times its depth change per texel. That is the first candidate, not a conclusion.

## Part 1: which strips are sunlit in the truth

The strips:

| Strip | View | Where it is known from |
|---|---|---|
| The outer quarter of the far jamb of the nearest right side window | `rect-lamps`, the person's camera | `reference.md`, part 3. Already measured: a leak. |
| The sill of the end window, which the ceiling reflects | `rect-lamps`, the same camera | `ter-ukw6`, and `stage-1b/pictures.md`, part 1 |
| The sill strips of the cabin | The cabin of `demo-exit`, `stills-cabin.json` | The handoff `docs/handoffs/2026-11-14-renderer-lab.md`. Find each strip in a still with the screen tracer off. |

For each strip, bring three measurements of one crop at one fixed exposure:

1. The engine's still with `--screen-trace off` under full lights, and the same without the sun. Their difference is the engine's direct sunlight.
2. A path trace of the exported frame with the sun and a black Earth (`REF_EARTH_BLACK=1`). It is the truth's direct sunlight plus its bounces.
3. The geometry's answer: cast a line from points across the strip to the sun through the exported triangles of the shadow casters. Count the points that the line reaches the sun from. This needs no renderer, so it is the judge where the two images disagree.

The result is one row per strip: sunlit in the truth, or only in the engine. A strip can be partly both, so give the width of each part in m and in pixels.

## Part 2: the cause of the leak

For each strip that is a leak, find which part of the shadow's approximation lets the light through. Change one part at a time with a probe switch, and measure the strip's width and its light:

| Part | Switch to add to the probe | What a cause looks like |
|---|---|---|
| The slope-scaled bias of the casters, `ShadowBias.SlopeBias` = 2.5 | Values 0, 1 and 2.5 | The strip narrows with the bias |
| The constant bias of the cascades, half a texel | 0 and 0.5 | The strip narrows with the bias |
| The normal offset of the lookup, one texel times the sine | 0 and 1 | The strip moves with the offset |
| The filter's reach, 4 × 4 taps | One tap | The strip's edge turns hard and moves by up to 2.5 texels |
| The texel size of the cascade that the strip reads | The cascade's index and texel size at the strip, from a dump | The strip's width is a fixed count of texels at three output sizes or camera distances |
| The caster set | Whether the wall above the opening is a caster in that cascade | The strip stays with every bias at 0 |

Also dump the shadow map's depth along a line across the strip, with the receiver's depth beside it. The record then shows the leak as numbers: the caster's stored depth, the receiver's depth, and the gap that the bias opens.

State the cause in one sentence that a reader can check, with its evidence. If two parts each cause a share of the strip, give the share of each.

## Part 4: one check for the Earth-light ticket (`t1-tbz9`)

The path trace's rooms are 16 to 58 times as bright as the engine's under full lights, and the reading is that Earth lights them. Two crops raise a doubt: `stage-2/earth-light-rect-lamps.png` and `stage-2/earth-light-cabin.png`. In `rect-lamps`, the engine shows black through the side window's pane, and the path trace shows blue there. In the cabin, the top right corner is bright blue in the engine and dark blue in the path trace. So the two renderers do not agree on what the camera sees of Earth.

Find out which is right, with the tools that you already hold:

- Where is Earth in the engine's frame, and where is it in the exported scene? Compare the direction, the distance and the radius.
- Take one engine still at a low fixed exposure that does not clip a directly seen Earth pixel. Compare its radiance with the path trace's at the same pixel, and with the expected value, albedo × 1,361 W/m² × cos / π.
- State whether the factors 16 and 58 stand, and add the result to `t1-tbz9` in your report's notes. The line orchestrator writes it into the ticket.

This part is small. Do it last, and do not let it delay parts 1 and 2.
