# On a slab deeper than 10 %, the fade by the entry share decides whether a recess between thick pillars brightens or darkens: get a reference before the rule is fixed

Found in stage 1b (docs/research/back-face-depth-layer/stage-1b/pictures.md, part 2, the cabin of demo-exit; image pictures-cabin-window-frames.png). Under the 10 % rule the jamb between two pillars of the left wall is a black strip. Under the layer as the slab it is mid-gray: +19.8 of 255 on the mean over 120 tiles, peak +43.8. With the fade computed over at most the first 10 % of depth (the probe's +fade switch), and the same hit decision, most of the brightening turns darker: 696 darker tiles against 42 brighter. So the fade rule, 1 - entryShare over the slab, sets the direction of the change on a thick slab. A hit deep inside a thick slab takes the light of the front pixel, which is the light of a face that the ray does not reach. No reference image exists, so nobody can say which column is right. Render a reference of the cabin's left wall (tools/Tertius.ReflectionReference or a path tracer), and compare the three columns with it.

## Acceptance Criteria

A reference image of the cabin's left wall exists beside the three columns, and the design states the fade rule for a slab deeper than 10 % with that evidence.
