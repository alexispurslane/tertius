# On a slab deeper than 10 %, the fade by the entry share decides whether a recess between thick pillars brightens or darkens: get a reference before the rule is fixed

Found in stage 1b (docs/research/back-face-depth-layer/stage-1b/pictures.md, part 2, the cabin of demo-exit, image pictures-cabin-window-frames.png). Under the 10 % rule the jamb between two pillars of the left wall is a black strip. Under the layer as the slab it is mid-gray: +19.8 of 255 on the mean over 120 tiles, peak +43.8. The probe's +fade switch computes the fade over at most the first 10 % of depth, with the same hit decision. Most of the brightening then turns darker: 696 darker tiles against 42 brighter. So the fade rule, 1 - entryShare over the slab, sets the direction of the change on a thick slab. A hit deep inside a thick slab takes the light of the front pixel. That is the light of a face that the ray does not reach. No reference image exists, so nobody can say which column is right. Render a reference of the cabin's left wall (tools/Tertius.ReflectionReference or a path tracer), and compare the three columns with it.

## Design of the experiment (stage 2, 2026-11-15)

This ticket is an experiment of stage 2, with no code for the branch. It uses the probe of `docs/research/back-face-depth-layer/stage-1b/probe.md` and the stills method of `stage-1b/pictures.md`. Read both first, and the record's section "Stage 1b".

### Terms

- A reference is an image of the same view that does not come from the screen walk. Its light at a point does not depend on what the screen shows.
- The hit decision is the walk's answer to one question: is the ray blocked here. The chosen model takes it from the real back face.
- The hit light is the light that the walk gives a blocked ray. Today it is the light of the hit pixel's visible face, mixed with the miss light by the entry share.
- The miss light is the light of a ray that nothing on screen blocks.
- The plane span of a pixel is the depth that the pixel's own surface plane covers over a square footprint around the pixel's center. Its half-width a is in pixels. With a = 0.5 the footprint is the pixel's own square.

### Part 1: the reference

Make a reference of two views, at the cameras and lights of the stage 1b stills:

1. The cabin of `demo-exit`, with the left wall's window frames and the recess between two pillars (`stage-1b/pictures-cabin-window-frames.png`).
2. `rect-lamps`, with the nearest right side window, its jamb, the floor patch below it, and the far ceiling (`stage-1b/pictures-rect-lamps-side-window.png` and `pictures-rect-lamps-ceiling.png`).

The first candidate is an offline path trace of the same geometry, lights and camera. The repository holds Blender in its assets shell (`flake.nix`, `tools/blender`), and Cycles is a path tracer. A throwaway export of the frame's meshes, instance placements, materials, lights and camera gives it the scene. The second candidate is a set of views from which the hidden faces are on screen. Choose one, and state in the record what the reference is and why it is true. State also where it differs from the engine by construction: the material model, the light units, the tone curve, the exposure.

Absolute levels will not match between an offline render and the engine. So compare ratios inside one image: the luminance of a named region over the luminance of a neutral region of the same image. Name the regions and give their rectangles.

### Part 2: the columns against the reference

Render these columns at one fixed exposure, with the settled frame of stage 1b, and judge each beside the reference:

| Column | Hit decision | Hit light |
|---|---|---|
| A | The 10 % rule | Today's: the front pixel's light, faded by the entry share over the 10 % slab |
| B | The layer as the slab | The front pixel's light, faded by the entry share over the real slab (`layer`) |
| C | The layer as the slab | The same, faded over at most the first 10 % (`layer+fade`) |
| D | The layer as the slab | The known-light rule below |

The known-light rule is a hypothesis of the parent agent. The measurement can break it. If it does, say so plainly.

- A blocked ray's light is known only where the ray meets the visible face. That is where its entry w lies within the plane span of the hit pixel behind the pixel's center w.
- A ray that enters deeper is blocked by a hidden face. The screen does not hold that face's light. It gets the unknown-face light.
- The fade then does not depend on the slab's thickness, and no 10 % constant returns.

Column D has two parameters, and each value needs a reason:

- The footprint's half-width a: 0.5 pixel (the pixel's own square) and 1.5 pixels (one neighbor more on each side). The reason for 1.5 is the finest level, which stores one flat depth per pixel. So a ray can cross the true surface up to one pixel before the walk sees it.
- The unknown-face light has three candidates. The miss light is today's value at the slab's back: the light of a ray that is not blocked. Black means that the hidden face gives nothing. The front pixel's light means that the hidden face is lit like the visible face, which is column B. Add one more candidate only with a reason of its own.

The probe needs a new switch for column D. Its signature, in the walk's result and in `traceFrom`:

```glsl
// Whether the ray's entry into the hit pixel's slab lies on the visible face: within the plane span of the hit
// pixel over a footprint of half-width `footprint` pixels, behind the pixel's center w.
bool entersVisibleFace(ivec2 hitPixel, float entryW, float footprint);

// In traceFrom, under the known-light rule:
result.light = entersVisibleFace(hitPixel, entryW, footprint) ? hitLightOf(hitPixel, kind) : unknownFaceLight;
```

`pixelPlane` of `depth_pyramid.glsl` gives the plane's gradient per pixel. The walk must hand out the entry w, which it computes already. Keep the screen-edge fade `edgeFade` as it is.

For each view, report for each column and the reference: the ratio of each named region, and what the eye sees. Then say which column is nearest to the reference in each region, and whether one rule is nearest in all of them.

### Part 3: the bands on the jamb

Under the layer, the jamb of the side window of `rect-lamps` shows uneven vertical bands. Name their cause with a measurement. One candidate is a step of the back share between neighbor pixels. Others are the cells of a pyramid level, and the fade over a long slab at a grazing angle. The last is noise of few samples that the false light hid before. Say whether the reference shows them. Say whether they are a fault of the layer or the true image without the false light.

- The reference's tools, the export and the probe's new switch are throwaway. They stay in patches, scratch commits and the folder `docs/research/back-face-depth-layer/stage-2/`. The branch gets documents, images, scripts and small tools only.
