# In mars-day the sun's edges in cascades 2 and 3 ramp over 0.48 m and 3.5 m, the cascade's texel, against a true penumbra under 0.21 m

Found by the shadow softness measure (t1-af5w, record docs/research/t1-af5w-shadow-softness/README.md, 'A finding for its own ticket').

The sun's shadow ramp is 1.5 texels of the cascade that holds the pixel (design L: lightOutsideShadow takes the share from 0 to 1 between lit fractions 5/8 and 1). In cascade 0 that is 0.088 m. The cascades' texels grow with distance, so the ramp is 0.19 m in cascade 1, 0.48 m in cascade 2 and 3.5 m in cascade 3.

In mars-day (Mars' sun, full angle 0.0061 rad) the sun's 11,669 edge pixels split almost evenly over the four cascades: 2,896, 2,914, 2,970 and 2,889. Their gaps are 0.21, 2.8 and 34 m (10th, 50th and 90th percentile). Their true widths (gap x angle) are 0.0013, 0.017 and 0.21 m. The rendered width over the true width is 7.4, 21 and 130. So a quarter of the view's sun edges ramp over 3.5 m, where the penumbra is at most about 0.2 m.

This is a cascade texel limit, not the gap-dependent softness of t1-af5w, so the measure leaves it out of that ticket's numbers. A fix is a question of the cascades' split or resolution at the view's distances, not of the filter's ramp.
