# The back-face pass: the back share of each pixel, drawn after the G-buffer pass

Step 3 of the design `docs/plans/back-face-depth-layer.md`. Read that document first: its terms, decisions 4, 8 and 9, and "The interfaces". This ticket builds the real pass. Nothing reads its image yet, so the frame's image does not change.

## System design

The pass runs after the G-buffer pass and before the depth pyramid, in every frame that runs the screen tracer. It is the only writer of the back share image.

| State | Writer | Content |
|---|---|---|
| `RenderTargets.BackShare`, R16F at the render size | `BackFacePass` | For each pixel: the front distance over the distance of the nearest counted fragment. 0 where no fragment counts. Exactly 1 for a fragment at the front's own distance. |

What the pass draws, from the runs of ticket `t1-6ucc`. They are `PreparedScene.Opaque[range].BackFace`, a `BackFaceRuns` with the parts `ClosedOneSided` and `BothFaces`:

| Runs | Culling | Alpha test | A fragment counts where |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ClosedOneSided.Solid` | Front faces culled | None | It lies behind the front surface |
| `ClosedOneSided.Cutout` | Front faces culled | The G-buffer's cutout test | It lies behind the front surface |
| `BothFaces.Solid` | None | None | It lies at or behind the front surface |
| `BothFaces.Cutout` | None | The G-buffer's cutout test | It lies at or behind the front surface |

- The pass draws the three depth ranges from far to near, each with the range's projection and a cleared depth, as the G-buffer pass does. Its depth test keeps the nearest fragment that counts. The color clears once, to 0.
- A nearer range overwrites a farther one. That is right, because a fragment only counts behind the pixel's front surface, and the front surface is the nearest surface of all ranges.
- The cutout test samples as the G-buffer pass does, with the main view's mip bias. Otherwise a pixel at a strand's edge takes the surface behind it.
- The pass uses the frame's jittered projections, so the front and the back of a pixel come from one frame.
- The throwaway `BackFacePass` of commit bacd0680d, its shaders, its dump code and `RenderTargets.BackDistance` leave the tree in this ticket.

## Signatures

```csharp
/// <summary>After the G-buffer pass and before the depth pyramid: writes the back share of each pixel.</summary>
internal sealed class BackFacePass : RenderPass
```

The fragment rule, one function for both kinds of run:

```glsl
// The tolerance within which a fragment's distance counts as the front surface's own, as a share of that distance.
// It keeps a front face from counting as its own back face. It is a float tolerance, not a thickness.
const float OwnSurfaceShare = 1e-4;

// The back share of a fragment at `distance` m from the eye over a front surface at `front` m, or a negative value
// for a fragment that does not count. A run that draws both faces counts a fragment at the front's own distance and
// gives it the share 1.
float backShareOf(float distance, float front, bool bothFaces);
```

The pass's time shows in the timings under its own name, "back faces".

## Settled decisions

- R16F, not R32F: a required color target format, half the memory, and exact at 0 and at 1.
- The four pipelines above, not one pipeline whose fragment shader drops front faces. The probe built the second form. It costs 37 % more in the cabin on the RTX 2000 Ada (`docs/research/back-face-depth-layer/stage-2/crowd-cost.md`, part 2).
- The pass draws every instance of its runs, also every skinned figure. The design's decision 8 has the cost model and the reasons.
- The pass reads positions through the vertex input of the G-buffer pass. A second vertex path with packed positions saved 0.019 ms of 0.489 ms and is not built.
- The probe's passes are in `docs/research/back-face-depth-layer/stage-1b/probe-slab-in-pyramid.patch` and `stage-2/probe-cards.patch`. They are starting points, not the code.
- Write to the Vulkan spec. The pass needs no new device feature and no blending.
- A depth range with no draw of the pass begins no rendering instance. On the M1 Max, three rendering instances that draw nothing cost about 0.075 ms (bug ticket `t1-s961`). The color still clears once per frame.

## Tests first

GPU tests on small scenes, each with a readback of the back share:

- A closed box in front of the sky: a pixel's share is its front distance over the distance of the box's back face.
- A two-sided card and an open quad: the share is exactly 1 on their pixels.
- A two-sided card in front of a box: the card's pixels hold 1, not the box's back face.
- The sky holds 0. A surface with nothing behind it and no back face of its own holds 0.
- A camera inside a closed mesh: the mesh's faces around the eye do not count for the surfaces beyond them.
- A cutout card: a pixel that shows the surface behind a hole holds that surface's share, also at a strand's edge.
- An object in the near range in front of an object in the far range: each pixel holds the back face of its own front surface.
- Two frames with different jitter: each frame's share matches its own front distance.

| Finding | Resolution |
