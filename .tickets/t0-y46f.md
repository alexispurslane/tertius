# Find why the regional lag test with the 18-draw window shows more light than the reference in a still

An experiment with the scratch probe. It changes nothing in src. It follows the cycle measurement (t0-mzhr), whose record left this cause unmeasured.


## Design (line orchestrator, 2026-11-14)

### What the cycle measurement found

The record is `docs/research/t0-mzhr-regional-test-cycle/README.md`. Read it first. It defines every term that this ticket uses, and its scratch patch holds every switch.

The regional lag test with a fast estimate over a box window of 18 draws ("region, window 18") trails least of all builds in the films. But in a still it shows more light than the reference. The reference is the scratch build with both history clamps off (`TERTIUS_A_NO_CLAMP=1`). These numbers are light as % of the reference under the same jitter:

| Take | Region, window 18 | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Lamp hall still, walls, no jitter | 106.5 | 99.9 |
| Lamp hall still, walls, today's jitter | 105.8 | 99.5 |
| Lamp hall still, ceiling, no jitter | 100.8 | 96.9 |
| Cabin, center region r11 before the switch, no jitter | 107.5 | 100.6 |
| Cabin, r11 before the switch, today's jitter | 110.0 | 99.4 |

The excess also exists without jitter, so the jitter's cycle does not cause it. Nobody measured its cause. A remedy that uses the regional test cannot be judged while the cause is unknown: either the test adds light that is not there, or the reference lacks light that is there.

### The parts and what causes what

- The history of a pixel owns its length in draws (the alpha of the slow history image). The regional test shortens the length, down to the fast cap of 4 draws.
- The ray plan reads the length. A pixel whose length is below `FullRateFrames` (8 draws, `screen_trace.glsl`) traces its own ray. Every other pixel takes the ray of its block's chosen pixel:

```glsl
if (historyLengthOf(r, kind) >= FullRateFrames && fits(p, c, kind))
    continue;
```

where

- `historyLengthOf(r, kind)` = the length in draws of the pixel's history of the ray kind (reflection or diffuse), read through its reprojection `r`.
- `fits(p, c, kind)` = true where the block's chosen pixel `c` lies on a surface like the pixel `p`'s, so that the pixel can use the block's ray.
- `continue` = the pixel takes the block's ray. Otherwise the plan appends an own ray for it.

- The resolve weights each ray at a pixel, and caps the weight at `MostRayWeight` (4.0, `screen_trace.glsl`). `screen_trace_resolve.comp` says that the weight is 1 for a pixel's own ray. So a block ray can lose light at the cap, and an own ray cannot.

So the regional test changes two things at once: the weights of the samples in the history (a short history weights its newest samples more), and which rays produce the samples (own rays in place of block rays). The reference has long histories, so nearly all of its samples come from block rays.

### Three candidate causes

1. Own rays carry more light than block rays, and the own rays are right. Then the reference is low by the excess, for example because of the weight cap or of the `fits` test, and every "% of the reference" in the lab's records is low by the same share where histories are long.
2. Own rays carry more light than block rays, and the block rays are right. Then the own-ray path adds light, and every young history is too bright for its first 8 draws.
3. The rays do not matter. The test shortens a history exactly when its region's newest samples are bright, so bright samples get large weights and dark samples get small ones. This is a selection bias (a mean that is wrong because the data choose their own weights).

### The switches

The scratch patch of the cycle measurement already holds what the takes need:

- `TERTIUS_X_FULL_RATE=<N>` sets `FullRateFrames`. The value 0 means that no pixel traces its own ray because of a short history. A value above the history cap, such as 1000, means that every pixel that starts a ray kind traces its own ray in every draw.
- `TERTIUS_X_REGION=8 TERTIUS_X_REGION_TOL=<percent> TERTIUS_X_REGION_WINDOW=18` is region, window 18.
- `TERTIUS_A_NO_CLAMP=1` is the reference. `TERTIUS_X_LIGHT_ACTION=cut` is the fix.
- `TERTIUS_DEBUG_JITTER=none` turns the jitter off. `TERTIUS_PROBE_EXPOSURE=4.08` fixes the lamp hall's exposure.

One addition is yours to shape: a count of the history lengths in the still state. For each signal part and each of the regions that `light.py` measures (ceiling, walls), it gives the share of pixels whose length is below 8 draws and the mean length, as a mean over at least 36 draws. A read-back in the style of `TERTIUS_X_CENSUS` fits, and so does a probe view of the length that a script reduces. Tool code only, in the scratch checkout.

### The takes

The frame of every take is the lamp hall still `rect-lamps` at 4.08 stops, with the long mean over the last 20 captures, measured by `light.py` against the plain reference of the same jitter. Take everything without jitter first, because the effect exists there without the cycle's teeth. Then repeat variants A to D under today's jitter.

| Variant | Switches | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| A, the reference | clamps off | The base of every share, 100 % by definition |
| B, the own-ray reference | clamps off, `TERTIUS_X_FULL_RATE=1000` | The light when every sample is an own ray and no clamp or test acts. If B stands above A, own rays carry more light than block rays |
| C, region, window 18 | as in the cycle measurement | Must repeat 100.8 / 106.5 without jitter and 99.5 / 105.8 under today's jitter. This is the control run of the new build |
| D, region, window 18, no own rays | C plus `TERTIUS_X_FULL_RATE=0` | If the excess goes away, the own rays cause it. If it stays, the rays do not matter |
| E, region, window 18, all own rays | C plus `TERTIUS_X_FULL_RATE=1000` | With B, it separates the test's effect from the rays' effect: E against B is the test alone on own rays |
| F, the fix, all own rays | the fix plus `TERTIUS_X_FULL_RATE=1000` | Whether the clamps cut the own rays' extra light |
| G, tolerance sweep | D at tolerances 20, 40 and 80 % | Only if D keeps an excess above 2 points: a selection bias falls as the test acts less often |

Also take the history-length count for C and for the fix. It tells how much of C's still the own rays light.

If B stands more than 2 points above A, find which of the two is right, as far as a still can tell. Two checks are cheap, and each is one more take:

- The weight cap: variant A with `MostRayWeight` raised to a value that never binds (add a scratch switch). If A rises to B, the cap takes the light from the block rays.
- The `fits` test: nothing to take. Report it as the remaining candidate if the cap does not explain the gap.

A second scene makes sure that the finding is not the lamp hall's alone: the still `exit-cabin` with variants A to D, without jitter, at a fixed exposure that you choose so that the image does not clip. Measure its whole image and its center region.

### The three questions

1. Do the own rays of short histories cause the excess of region, window 18? Give D against C, and the history-length count.
2. Do own rays carry more light than block rays when no test and no clamp act? Give B against A. If yes, give the weight-cap take and say which side a still supports.
3. What excess remains that the rays do not explain, and does it fall with the tolerance? Give D, E against B, and the sweep if it ran.
