# Per-tile light budget: a tile with more than K lights drops its faintest lights while they hold at most 2 % of its light

The user's decision of 2026-11-04: with light sampling removed (ter-9nrc), every light of the main view is shaded exactly, and its cost grows with each light that a pixel loops over. Build light culling per screen tile now, so that the exact loop and the forward passes of the main view loop only over the lights whose reach touches the tile. Design first: the orchestrator presents two designs (for example 2D tiles with depth bounds from the G-buffer against 3D clusters) after a fact-finding read of every consumer of the light list, the depth layout and the exact loop's cost. Acceptance: lamp-hall-64 costs no more than the removed sampling did (1280 x 720, at the windowed clock), demo-exit's deck costs no more than the exact loop without tiles, and the images equal the exact loop's.

2026-11-04, deferred by the user: light budget only for now. A read-only fact-finding (GPT 6.1 SOL, /private/tmp/tile-lights-facts.md) found that in lamp-hall-64 every lamp's reach covers the whole hall: ARCHITECTURE.md records a 2.5 W hall lamp reaching about 130 m at about +5 stops, and the grid spans about 32 m. Reach is where a light's pre-exposed radiance falls below 2^-13 (LightUnits.cs), and walls do not shorten it. So tile or cluster lists cannot drop a lamp there, and they help only scenes whose lights are spread out beyond their reach, such as spin-station (129 lamps). Other facts for when this ticket returns: main-view lists can serve the direct pass (2D tiles with depth bounds from the G-buffer's distance target) or also glass and fringes (3D clusters); lit puffs (vertex-lit at their centers, which can lie outside the view), environment faces and impostor captures keep the full loop. The exact loop sums in light-buffer order (lighting.glsl:411), so lists must keep that order for identical images; an atomic append does not. Memory at 1920 x 1080 with 16 x 16 tiles: 2 to 8 MiB for 2D tiles with a cap of 64 to 256 lights, 32 to 256 MiB for dense clusters of 16 to 32 slices. Published assignment costs: Olsson et al. 2012 on a GTX480, 32 lights 0.71 ms clustered against 0.24 ms tiled; Harada 2012's 2.5D mask adds under 10 %. The engine has no fixed cap on the light list. Trigger to resume: a real scene whose lights are spread out and whose exact loop is too slow for the 12 ms target. Then measure first: count the lights that each tile and each cluster keeps on that scene before any design. The light budget itself comes from ter-9nrc's measurement of the exact loop's cost per scene; the engine enforces no limit.

**2026-11-04T20:57:13Z**

2026-11-04, the user's idea for this design: a reach from what the eye can notice against the light already at a pixel (a relative, Weber-like threshold), instead of the absolute cutoff R_cut = 2^-13 (half an 8-bit step on black). The orchestrator's reading: a relative threshold depends on the other lights at the pixel, so it is a per-tile decision, not a per-light reach; it is the same idea as this ticket's cap (drop what is faint against what the tile keeps), with an energy form: drop the faintest lights at a tile while their summed bound stays under a fraction of the tile's light. Estimates for lamp-hall-64 at +4.56 stops: a 2.5 W lamp reaches about 111 m today; a cutoff at 1 % of a mid-grey floor (about 15 times R_cut) gives about 29 m, still about the hall's 32 m; a tile that keeps only lamps within 10 m needs a cutoff about 120 times R_cut, and under an even grid of lamps at height h the lamps beyond horizontal distance R give about h/R of the floor's light (about 30 % at h = 3 m, R = 10 m), so that light is lost however smooth the edge. Before the design: measure, on the hall and spin-station, the lights that each tile keeps under (1) today's reach, (2) a per-light relative threshold of 1 % and 2 %, (3) an energy budget of 2 % and 5 % of the tile's light, (4) the K strongest; then present two designs (the cap as decided, and the energy-budget form) with those counts and the lost light.

**2026-11-04T21:09:44Z**

2026-11-04, the user's decision: the per-tile cap becomes an energy budget. At each tile, rank the lights by their light at the tile and drop them from the faintest upward while the dropped light stays at or below 2 % of the tile's whole light; if more than the ceiling still remain, keep dropping the faintest until the ceiling holds (the user: 'perhaps reusing K or M'; the orchestrator's reading: M, the view cap's most shaded lights, so a tile never shades more than M lights, and only a tile with more than M near-equal lights loses more than 2 %). So the kept set is the fewest strongest lights that hold at least 98 % of the tile's light, but never more than M. Open points for the design (designer agent, after the measurement of the earlier note): (1) the light estimate of a light at a tile (a bound over the tile's depth range, or a value at representative points) and what the 2 % then guarantees per pixel; (2) seams: neighbouring tiles can drop different light, up to 2 % each, which sits at the eye's threshold of about 1 to 2 % on a smooth floor, so the design must measure the step at tile edges and say how it hides it (a fade by the light's place in the budget, a smaller budget, or jittered tiles that the temporal passes average); (3) pops in time: no per-light memory per tile, so a light that crosses the budget pops at that tile, also at the threshold; (4) the view cap (rule A, ter-bqun) stays the outer bound and the rule of the readers without tiles (lit puffs, impostor captures), because the CPU has no light per pixel to budget; the design states why the two rules differ. Acceptance to settle in the design, at least: the hall's look close to the uncapped hall's, spin-station's far lamps keep their light, the deck unchanged, no visible tile grid in a still or a film.

**2026-11-04T21:11:58Z**

2026-11-04, the user's correction of the rule above: a tile drops nothing while it has at most the target number of lights; past it, it drops the faintest lights while the dropped light stays at or below 2 % of the tile's light, and then stops, even if it still has more than the target. The view cap (ter-bqun) bounds the worst case. The orchestrator's settlement: the target is K, not M. The tile lists hold only the lights that the main view keeps, and the view cap keeps at most M of them, so no tile can have more than M lights, and a target of M would never act. K means 'lights that are always shaded whole' in both rules. So a tile shades between its 2 % set and M lights. Expected, to be checked by the measurement: under an even grid of lamps at height h, the lamps beyond horizontal distance R hold about h/R of the floor's light, so in lamp-hall-64 a 2 % budget drops few lamps (only those beyond about 18 m at h = 3 m), and there the view cap's M still does most of the cutting; the budget pays where lights are spread out, as in spin-station. With tile lists in place, M's default must be measured again (most tiles then shade fewer lights than M).
