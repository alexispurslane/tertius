# A spot lamp is in the main view only when its cone reaches the view

Found by ter-ox1a's fixer and the orchestrator (2026-10-31). PreparedLight.InMainView is !camera.Culls(relative, reach) (SceneBatcher.cs:1019), a sphere of the light's reach, and LightUnits.EyeIrradiance, the shadow tiles' ranking value, is intensity over distance squared with no regard to a spot lamp's axis. So a spot lamp that points away from the camera counts as in view: it stays in the direct pass's loop, and it can take a shadow tile from a lamp that lights the floor in view. Example from ter-ox1a: a 1-layer atlas, lamp X 30 m behind the eye aimed away, lamp Z 60 m ahead lighting the floor; one stop brighter, X's reach sphere holds the eye, X's eye irradiance is 4 times Z's, and Z's shadow turns off while Z's light stays. Fix in the one existing flag: a spot lamp's main-view test uses its cone, cut at its reach, against the view frustum; decide with the user whether the tiles' ranking value also accounts for the axis. Expected gains: fewer lamps in the direct pass's loop, and no tile for a lamp whose light cannot show. Test first with the ter-ox1a example scene.

**2026-10-31T19:16:07Z**

2026-10-31, settled implementation choice (builder), commit 1, the cone test: CameraView.Culls(apex, cone, reach) tests the cut cone (points within the reach of the lamp and within the outer half-angle of its axis) against the same five planes as the sphere test (near plane, four side planes, no far plane). For each plane it adds reach times the most that a unit step along a cone direction goes into the plane: 1 when the plane's normal lies within the outer half-angle of the axis, else cos(phi - theta), floored at 0. The test is exact per plane and conservative across planes: a cut cone that misses the view near an edge or a corner where two planes meet, yet lies outside neither plane alone, stays in the main view, the same error as the sphere test. A cone of a whole turn culls as the sphere. The faces of the environment map keep the sphere test for every light (out of scope: the ticket names the main view).

**2026-10-31T20:49:57Z**

2026-10-31, settled decision (orchestrator, from the user's own decisions of 2026-10-31): a spot lamp whose cone reaches the main view but whose axis misses the view (NearestInView returns null) ranks for shadows in its own tier. The tier order is: 1. main-view lights whose beam (spot lamp, axis in view) or position (point light) is in view; 2. main-view spot lamps whose axis misses the view; 3. lights that only a face keeps (InMainView false), as before. Inside each tier the order is as before: the higher RankIrradiance first, ties in list order. A lamp of tier 2 keeps RankDistance = reach, so inside tier 2 it ranks by its light at its reach. Reason: the user's design B gives such a lamp 'the lowest rank among lights in the main view', because its strongest light falls outside the view. The user rejected design A because a lamp whose beam only clips the view's edge can take a tile from one that lights the floor ahead. D = reach alone does not give the lowest rank. The reviewer's scene: a 1-tile atlas at -10 stops; lamp 1 at (12, 0, -5), aimed +X, 60 degree cone, 30 m reach (its cone clips the right edge, its axis misses the view) ranks at 1.0 cutoff; lamp 2 at (0, 3, -40), aimed down at the floor in view, 25 m reach, ranks at 0.39 cutoffs (D = 40 m). Before, TileHolders gave [1]; the settled rule gives [2]. Rejected: RankDistance = +infinity with ties in list order, because then FarShadows' Share threshold never gives such a far lamp a slot.

**2026-10-31T20:49:57Z**

2026-10-31, settled implementation choices (builder), the tier: (1) One store. PreparedLight.InMainView (a bool) became PreparedLight.Tier, an enum MainViewTier { FaceOnly = 0, AxisMissesView = 1, InView = 2 }; a higher value ranks first. InMainView stays as a derived read (Tier != FaceOnly) for CollectLights.Reaches, GpuLight, ImpostorManager and the docs. The zero value is FaceOnly, so a default PreparedLight is outside the main view, as the default bool was. SceneBatcher.MainViewAndRank writes the tier and RankDistance together, from the same test. (2) Every main-view point light is tier 1 (InView), whether or not its position is in view: its rank is its irradiance at the eye, as before, and tier 2 names only spot lamps. (3) One order. ShadowTiles.Candidate.Ranked compares the tier first (higher first), then RankIrradiance, then list order. FarShadows.Choose makes one choice per tier, the highest first, each from the cascade sets that the tiers above leave, with the threshold Share x the brightest far light of that tier and the tiers above it: the tiers above light the same view, so beside a light 256 times brighter a lower tier's shadow cannot show, and no lower tier counts, so a lower tier cannot take a slot from a higher one. That is the old rule for the first and the last tier. (4) A far spot lamp is rare but possible: a lamp of 2 x 10^14 W/sr reaches 2.3 x 10^7 m at -10 stops, so FarShadowsTests test the tier order with hand-built PreparedLight values. (5) Tests: SpotLampViewTests gain the reviewer's scene (tile to lamp 2) and a point light 30 m ahead with a 20 m reach against a tier-2 lamp in a 6-layer atlas (the point light keeps its six tiles); FarShadowsTests gain four tier tests. All four new ranking tests failed before the change (the tier-2 lamp took the tile or the set). No existing test changed its expected answer; tests that set InMainView now set Tier.
