# Reflections: find and attribute every artifact on demo-exit and the review scenes

Diagnosis ticket of docs/plans/screen-space-artifacts.md (section 'What a diagnosis delivers'). Known artifacts: the blocky fragments of the greenhouse's reflection on the deck (captures/artifacts-2026-11-01/deck-reflection-blocks.png), and the stair steps, hard cut lines and floating green line on the floor film (ter-eqtb, captures/artifacts-2026-11-01/floor-reflection-steps.png). Starts after ter-rjts merges, because ter-rjts changes the reflection passes. Build a gallery of captures at 1920 x 1080 (frames of each of demo-exit's nine films, and the review scenes of docs/plans/screen-space-reflections.md: reflections, mirror-balls, pbr-spheres, demo-surface, hair-rugs), find every artifact of the reflections, and attribute each to its stage (depth pyramid, radiance pyramid, half-resolution trace, upsample, own rays, cone, fallback) with probes and the A/B switches. No fix goes into the code.

## Acceptance Criteria

A ticket note lists every artifact found, with its crop, its stage, the probe evidence, whether it is a fault or a limit of the method, and the candidate fixes with their cost. The probes are not in the commits.

Artifact 1: stair steps and sawtooth ramps on surfaces that the eye sees at a grazing angle (builder, 2026-11-01, revised in the fix stage). This is ter-eqtb's "stair steps", and part of its "hard cut lines".
- What shows: horizontal bands several pixels tall. Inside each band the reflection fades from full to almost nothing, and at the next band it jumps back to full. So the bands read as stairs or as a sawtooth.
  - films/floor: the floor's reflection of the far floor where it curves up, x 450-1400, y 560-720 (sheets/floor-steps-refs-2x.png, shot-floor-steps.png). The band edges near y 565-590 are the "hard cut lines" of ter-eqtb.
  - films/chute-top: the slide's reflection, x 820-1160, y 560-860 (sheets/chute-top-slide-1.5x.png).
  - films/deck and films/unbelt: the same ramps inside the reflected greenhouse and cabin frame (sheets/deck-vizclass-1.5x.png, sheets/unbelt-lobe-1x.png, vizclass panel). There artifact 3 dominates.
- Stage: the trace's level-0 hit test and its entry share q (reflection.glsl, traceRay, the branch "meetsSlab && level == 0u"). The same code runs at full resolution. Mechanism: a level-0 pixel is a slab of one inverse depth, its center's, from w down to w / (1 + kappa) (decision 16). A surface that the eye sees at a grazing angle spans a wide range of depths inside one pixel. A ray that crosses such a surface enters the next row's flat slab partway. So q at the pixel's edge rises from 0 to 1 over a band of neighboring origin rows. Then the rays pass that row and hit the next row with q = 0. The weight 1 - q makes the ramp, and the row change makes the jump.
- Probe evidence:
  - vizclass shows the ramp of 1 - q inside each band and its jump at each band edge (sheets/floor-steps-vizclass-4x.png).
  - vizpos shows that each band is one hit row (G constant per band, same sheet).
  - noq (q = 0 everywhere) removes the steps (sheets/floor-steps-refs-2x.png).
  - ref-full, ref-steps1024, ref-full1024 and level0walk keep them unchanged. So neither the half-resolution trace, nor the step limit, nor the depth pyramid's coarse levels cause them. vizclass shows no ray that runs out of steps there (no red).
  - kappa001 makes them finer and darker, with gaps (same sheet). So the slab model of the pixel is involved, not the thickness's size.
  - plane0, which treats each level-0 pixel as its plane from the G-buffer's geometric normal, removes most of them at both resolutions (sheets/floor-steps-plane0-2x.png). vizclass-plane0 shows q = 0 on almost every such hit (sheets/floor-cutlines-plane0-2x.png).
  - lobenorm shows no bands (sheets/fix/floor-steps-planeext-1x.png).
- Noise or bias: bias. It is the same in every run and at every reference.
- Fault or limit: a fault of the design's level-0 model, which the code implements as designed. The screen holds the information that removes it: the G-buffer's geometric normal gives the plane of each pixel. So it is not a limit of screen-space methods.
- Candidate fixes. Each one changes the image, so each is a design decision for the user. Costs: the four reflection passes' summed mean by the renderer's timer, over 120 frames. probes/timing.sh runs interleaved rounds in rotated order with a control build (base2, a byte copy of base). The fix stage timed six deck rounds and five floor rounds at a 1-minute load of 15 to 42 (timing/fix-stage.txt). Medians: deck base 3.78, control 3.81 ms. Floor base 3.43, control 3.53 ms. The control's medians lie within 0.1 ms of base.
  - A. Level-0 pixels as planes, the model defined once (probe planeext, review finding F6). The fix ticket defines a pixel's depth extent once: the nearest and the farthest inverse depth of its plane over the pixel's square, with the slab behind it. pixelBounds returns that extent, so depth_pyramid_first builds level 1 from it and every coarser level bounds the same surface that the level-0 plane test sees. Then no coarse cell can skip a plane that leans out of a flat bound. plane0's split model allows that class of bug.
    - Effect: it removes the remaining bands and the horizontal cut at y about 595 that plane0 keeps (sheets/fix/shot-floor-steps-planeext.png, sheets/fix/floor-steps-planeext-1x.png).
    - New structure to fix: dark vertical streaks on films/floor at x about 585, 760, 1100 and 1270, y 560-740. Also a dark sliver on the wall at x 920-945, y 0-130 (sheets/fix/floor-streaks-planeext-2x.png). The probe extends each pixel's plane over its whole square. At a depth edge, that plane reaches depths where no surface is, so rays hit it. The fix ticket must bound a silhouette pixel's extent, for example by its neighbors' depths. plane0's own streaks at x about 840 and 940, y 0-280 stay in planeext.
    - Cost of the unoptimized probe: deck 4.81 ms, +1.03 ms. Floor 4.16 ms, +0.74 ms. The depth pyramid pass does not grow (deck 0.61 against 0.63 ms). All of the growth is in the trace and the own rays, because the wider coarse bounds make more rays descend.
  - A2. plane0, the split model (planes at level 0, flat bounds above): deck 4.51 ms, +0.73 ms, floor 3.89 ms, +0.46 ms. It keeps faint bands and the cut at y about 595. Not recommended, because it holds two models of one pixel's depth extent (review finding F6).
  - B. q against the plane at the hit only (probe planeq): the steps become fainter and stay (sheets/floor-steps-planeq-2x.png). Cost: deck 3.752, 3.760 ms, about +0.09 ms (first stage's rounds). Not sufficient alone.
  - C. A fade by the hit surface's facing to the eye (probe grazefade): smoothstep(0.05, 0.2, |n_hit . view|). This is a mask of the fault, not a fix: it leaves the level-0 model as it is and drops the hits where the hit surface is grazing. On films/floor it removes the steps by dropping these hits to the map (sheets/floor-steps-grazefade-2x.png). It does not change artifact 1 on films/deck: inside the box x 940-1400, y 640-900 only 0.82 % of the pixels change by more than 4 levels, so the deck's ramps remain. On films/unbelt it changes 9.5 % of the box x 560-1500, y 520-1080, and the ramps that lie on surfaces that face the eye remain. Cost: inside the spread (first stage: deck 3.736 and 3.991 ms against base 3.721 and 3.983 ms in the same rounds). Price: it loses the far floor's reflection, and it adds hard-edged regions where the fade cuts (sheets/chute-top-slide-1.5x.png, grazefade panel).
  - D. No entry fade (probe noq): it removes the steps but gives a ray that passes behind an object full weight. It undoes decision 23's reason, so it is not a candidate on its own.
