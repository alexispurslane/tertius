# The front of every slab at the finest level is its plane's near w inside the pixel, so every level is the merge of the level below

**2026-11-25 · Retired.** The back-face depth and screen-ray study retains the probes. A future geometry or ray-hit change needs a production shot whose incorrect hit can be traced.

Later work of the back-face depth layer (`ter-kh4g`). Read `docs/plans/back-face-depth-layer.md`, decision 3, and `docs/research/back-face-depth-layer/stage-2/cards.md`, part 3. The parent agent decided on 2026-11-15 to plan this rule and not to build it in the epic.

## The model

A surface has one depth at each point. A pixel shows a piece of that surface, and a tilted piece covers a range of depths inside the pixel. So the honest front of a pixel's slab is that range, not one flat depth.

After the epic, the pyramid states the front of one pixel in two ways:

| Level | The front of a pixel's slab | The back of a pixel's slab |
|---|---|---|
| The finest level | The center w: one flat depth | `min(slabBack(centerW, share), planeFarW)` |
| The pixel's term at level 1 | The plane's near w inside the pixel | `slabBack(planeFarW, share)` |

This ticket removes the difference. The finest level holds the pixel's term of level 1. Every level is then the merge of the level below, and one function gives a pixel's slab.

The rule holds for every pixel, not for sheets only. The reasons:

- The flat depth is an approximation for every surface, not only for a sheet.
- The levels above the finest already start each pixel's slab at its plane's near w.
- A rule for sheets only needs a branch on the back share in the front of a slab. A pixel's front then has two meanings.

The gain shows on thin slabs. A ray that passes in front of a flat depth and behind the real surface is inside the solid. Under a deep slab, it meets the slab of the next cell, so the hit moves by one pixel at most. Under a sheet, or a thin plate at a grazing angle, it is behind the next cell's slab too, and the hit is lost.

## System design

| State | Writer | After the epic | After this ticket |
|---|---|---|---|
| The finest level of the depth pyramid | The pyramid's build | (center w, back w) | (the plane's near w, back w): the pixel's slab |
| The center w of a pixel | The G-buffer pass, as the front distance | Also the finest level's first part | The G-buffer's front distance only |

Three readers in `screen_trace.glsl` take a pixel's center w from the finest level today. Each one needs a source that still holds the center:

| Reader | What it needs |
|---|---|
| `hittable(ivec2 pixel)` | Whether the pixel holds a surface. The slab's front answers that as well: the sky's is 0. |
| `guidedPointOf(ivec2 pixel, ivec2 target)` | The point of the target pixel's center, for a guided diffuse ray |
| `pixelDensityOf(vec3 origin, vec3 direction, ivec2 q)` | The point of the pixel's center, for the density of a guided ray |

The walk's hit point is a fourth use: check what `walkScreen` returns as the hit's depth, and what `hitLightOf` and the entry share take from it.

The one-image depth pyramid (`ter-y3k4`) decides what mip 0 holds and who reads it. So this ticket follows it.

## Signatures

```glsl
// The slab of a pixel: x its plane's near w inside the pixel, y the back w of its slab from its plane's far w, or
// the empty bounds for sky. The finest level stores it, and each level above merges the level below.
vec2 pixelSlab(ivec2 pixel);
```

`pixelSlabBounds` goes, because `pixelSlab` gives the same pair. `slabBack(float w, float share)` does not change.

## The named risk, to measure first

The pair (near w, far w) of a plane inside a pixel is a box, and the plane fills only its diagonal. The box also holds empty space in front of the plane's far half. So a ray that grazes a surface can hit a neighbor's box in front of the real surface.

Measured on sheets only (stage-2/cards.md, part 3): the rule keeps 71 % to 92 % of the real hits, against 57 % to 90 % for the epic's rule. In the lamp hall's reflection it keeps 565 of the 10 % rule's 4,399 false hits, against 484.

Not measured: the rule on every pixel. Before the build, run the census of stage 2 with the rule on every pixel, in the lamp hall, `rect-lamps` and the deck. Count the false hits, and the hits of a ray within two pixels of its own start.

If the census names a misbehavior on deep slabs, the exception is "sheets and thin slabs only". Record it then with its number.
