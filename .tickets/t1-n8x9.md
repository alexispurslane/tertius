# THROWAWAY probe: the back layer as the screen walk's slab, stored in the depth pyramid (stage 1b)

This ticket builds one throwaway probe. The two other tickets of stage 1b measure with it: the pictures (t1-gxzg) and the cost (t1-stwp). The probe models the design that Alexis chose on 2026-11-14. Read docs/research/back-face-depth-layer/README.md first, mainly "The terms of this record", "The throwaway probe", "Two designs" and "The answers of Alexis".

## Terms

- w is the inverse view depth of a surface point, in 1/m. A larger w is nearer.
- The slab of a pixel or cell is the interval of w that the screen walk treats as solid there.
- The back share of a pixel is its front distance over the distance of the first back face behind it. It is 0 where no back face lies behind the front surface. `BackFacePass` writes it to `RenderTargets.BackDistance` (commit bacd0680d).
- The 10 % rule is main's slab: from w to w / (1 + `ThicknessShare`).
- The layer as the slab is the chosen rule: from the front w to the w of the real back face, also where that is farther than 10 %.
- The trimmed slab is the 10 % slab that ends at a nearer back face. It is an optional reference.
- A sheet is a surface pixel with no back face behind it. The chosen rule gives it no thickness.

## System design

One store holds the slab: the depth pyramid. Only the pyramid's build applies the slab rule. The walk reads a front w and a back w at every level and holds no thickness constant.

| State | Owner (the only writer) | Content after this ticket |
|---|---|---|
| The back share image, R32F at the render size | `BackFacePass` | Unchanged, with one change of what it draws (see "Settled decisions") |
| The depth pyramid's finest level | `depth_pyramid_finest.comp` | RG32F: R is the front w of the pixel's center, as today. G is the back w. Sky holds (0, 0). |
| The depth pyramid's levels 1 and above | `depth_pyramid_first.comp`, then the down and tail shaders | RG32F as today: R is the largest front w of the cell. G is the smallest back w of the cell's pixels. |
| The light pyramid | Its own shaders | Unchanged: its finest level stays R32F. |

What causes what:

1. The G-buffer pass writes the front distance. `BackFacePass` then writes the back share.
2. The finest shader reads the front distance and the back share of its pixel. It stores the center w and `slabBack(w, share)`.
3. The level-1 shader reads each pixel's bounds from `pixelBounds` and the pixel's back share. It replaces the far bound with `slabBack(farBound, share)` and then merges as today. The levels above merge with `mergedBounds`, unchanged: the smallest second part wins.
4. `walkScreen` takes `near` from the texel's first part and `back` from its second part, at every level. The multiply by `backShare` leaves the walk.

The invariant that the walk depends on stays: the slab of a cell holds the slab of every pixel under it. A pixel's far bound is at most its center w, and both get the same share.

## Signatures

In `thickness.glsl`, read by the pyramid's build only:

```glsl
const uint SlabTenPercent = 0u;   // main's rule, through the new store: the control
const uint SlabTrimmed = 1u;      // optional reference: the 10 % slab, ended at a nearer back face
const uint SlabLayer = 2u;        // the chosen rule

const uint NoBackSheet = 0u;      // the chosen rule: back w = front w
const uint NoBackSheetPlane = 1u; // a sheet as deep as its own plane over the pixel's square (finest level only)
const uint NoBackTenPercent = 2u; // main's rule for these pixels
const uint NoBackSolid = 3u;      // solid without end: back w = 0

// The w of the back of what is solid behind a surface at inverse depth `w`, whose back share is `share`
// (0 for no back face). `w` is a pixel's center w at the finest level and its plane's far bound at level 1.
float slabBack(float w, float share);
```

- `SlabTenPercent`: `w / (1 + ThicknessShare)`. `SlabTrimmed`: `max(w / (1 + ThicknessShare), w * share)`. `SlabLayer`: `w * share` for a share above 0, and the no-back rule otherwise.
- `NoBackSheetPlane` differs from `NoBackSheet` at the finest level only: the finest shader stores `min(w, pixelBounds(pixel).y)` as the back w. It exists because of the stair case below.
- How the two rules reach the shader is your choice: specialization constants, or one tree per rule with a constant in the source. Each pair of rules must build as a tree for a cloud job and as a frozen Release binary on the Mac.

The changed lines of the walk (`screen_walk.glsl`, sketched in the record under "A usage example of each"):

```glsl
cellBounds = level == 0 ? texelFetch(finest, ivec2(cell), 0).rg : texelFetch(bounds, ivec2(cell), level - 1).rg;
float near = cellBounds.x;
float back = cellBounds.y;
```

One more switch, for the pictures ticket: the fade over at most the 10 % slab. With it on, the walk computes the entry share against `max(back, near / (1 + ThicknessShare))` and not against `back`. The slab that decides a hit does not change. It shows what the fade `1 - entryShare` does on a slab that is thicker than today.

C# side: the depth pyramid's finest format becomes RG32F in `PyramidImages`. The back share image needs a binding for the two build shaders. The comment on `PyramidImages.FinestSet` says that binding 0 of the depth pyramid's finest set is unwritten. Make sure of that, and of the level-1 set, before you add a binding to a layout. The other readers of the finest level read `.r` and keep working.

## The stair case, which the probe must make measurable

The finest level stores one flat w per pixel, the center's. A surface that the camera sees at a grazing angle is then a stair: each pixel's step stands one step nearer or farther than its neighbor's. Call g the change of the surface's w per pixel along the ray's image, and h the change of the ray's w per pixel. With g > 2h > 0, a ray can stand in front of one step and behind the next, and cross no step. Today the 10 % slab catches it behind the next step. A sheet with no thickness does not. `NoBackSheetPlane` catches the crossings in the half of the pixel where the plane is farther than the center. A closed thin plate has the same stair once its thickness along the view ray is below one step. This is the check that the parent asked for: a ray must be able to hit a slab of zero thickness.

## Settled decisions

- The probe is throwaway. It adds no unit test to the branch and does not change the C# copies (`ScreenRay`, `DepthPyramid`, `CellBounds`). The controls below take the place of tests. I settled this as the line orchestrator: a probe for a measurement is an experiment.
- The branch stays green. Commit to the branch only files that pass the pre-commit hook unchanged: the patch, scripts and documents. Keep the probe's source changes in the patch and in scratch commits (`git commit-tree`, refs under `refs/scratch/ter-kh4g-1b-...`), as stage 1 did. Restore `src` before you commit.
- `BackFacePass` draws the one-sided solid runs only. Stage 1 drew one-sided cutout runs as solid, which puts back faces behind a cutout's holes. Two-sided runs and cutout runs stay out, so their pixels are sheets wherever nothing else lies behind them. Report how many draw commands of each kind the measured views hold.
- The probe leaves three readers of `ThicknessShare` as they are: `TargetDepthShare` in `screen_trace.glsl`, the plane tolerance in `screen_trace_plan.comp`, and the environment map's near entry. Say in the probe's document what each does under the layer as the slab.
- Mode names: reuse `TERTIUS_BACK_FACE_PROBE` and `TERTIUS_BACK_FACE_DUMP` where a switch at run time fits.

## Controls (the probe's acceptance)

1. Main twice: two headless runs of main's tree with one script on the Mac give the same image, byte for byte. This is the baseline of the next control. If they differ, report the size of the difference and use it as the tolerance.
2. `SlabTenPercent` against main: the same script gives the same image as main. The rule computes the same slab through the new store, and the multiply commutes with the minimum. If the images differ, find the reason before any other work.
3. `SlabLayer` dump of the finest level: G / R equals the pixel's back share on every surface pixel with a back face. G equals R on the sheets. At the sill of `rect-lamps` the slab is about 0.50 m (the record, "What the back layer holds").
4. The invariant: for every cell of level 1 in one dumped frame, G is at most the G of each pixel under it.
5. One still of `scripts/rect-lamps.json` per slab rule exists and you opened each. A trimmed film is fine.
