# A still's diffuse light depends on the diffuse history's length: a cap of 4 draws adds 2 to 7 % with the diffuse filter and removes 5 to 15 % without it

Found 2026-11-14 by t0-y46f (docs/research/t0-y46f-window-18-excess/README.md, sections "Question 3" and "Scene light").

In a still, the long mean of an exponential history of unbiased samples must not depend on the history's length. The scratch switch TERTIUS_X_DIFFUSE_CAP=<N> caps the diffuse history's length at N draws at every pixel, with no test. The takes are the lamp hall still rect-lamps at 4.08 stops, no jitter, with both clamps off (TERTIUS_A_NO_CLAMP=1). Each number is the light of the long mean as % of the same build with the cap of 32 draws, ceiling / walls. Scene light is the probe's log-luminance capture, with no tone curve. The survey measure is light.py, which inverts the tone curve on a mean of display luminance and bends noisy variants down.

- Cap 4, scene light: 102.3 / 105.0, whole image 101.9 %. Survey measure: 104.2 / 106.8, whole image 104.1 %.
- Cap 4, the bounce signal alone, survey measure: 104.3 / 107.9, whole image 105.2 %.
- Cap 8, survey measure: 103.7 / 106.0. Cap 16: 102.5 / 103.9.
- Cap 4 over 5,900 draws (long mean of draws 5,313 to 5,940), survey measure: 105.0 / 107.1 against the cap of 32 over the same draws. So the gap is steady. It is not a slow rise of the long history.
- Cap 4 with the diffuse filter off (TERTIUS_DIFFUSE_FILTER=off), against the cap of 32 with the filter off. Scene light: 94.8 / 89.8, whole image 84.9 %. Survey measure: 84.6 / 84.5. The sign changes in both measures.
- Cabin still exit-cabin at 1.0 stops, survey measure: cap 4 gives 101.8 % of the whole smooth image and 102.0 % of r11.

The clamps, the own rays and the ray weight cap do not cause it (t0-y46f takes B, D, E and Acap). The engine cuts a diffuse history to the fast cap of 4 draws after light evidence. So a young history after a light change carries this bias in its first draws. The sign change with the filter off suggests a length-dependent step in the diffuse filter's loop. That loop is the filter's feedback into the slow history and the rays that read the filtered light at their hits. A step of the accumulation itself is not ruled out. Measure in scene light, not with light.py.

## Notes

**2026-11-15T08:45:02Z**

Diagnosis design (line orchestrator, 2026-11-15). The ticket lead of t0-y46f continues with this diagnosis. No change under src. The output is a note here and the folder docs/research/t8-69v7-length-light/.

The model. In a still, each draw gives a pixel one sample of its bounce light. The slow history is a running mean of the samples with the weight 1 / length. A mean with any weight has the samples' own mean, as long as every step between the sample and the stored value is linear. So a light that depends on the length names a step that is not linear, or a loop whose gain depends on the noise. The engine has such a loop: a ray reads, at its hit, the last draw's bounce light (the filter's output, or the fast history with the filter off), so the samples depend on what the histories held one draw before.

The frame of every take: the lamp hall still rect-lamps at 4.08 stops, no jitter, both clamps off, measured in scene light (scene.py, scenelight.py) against the same variant with the cap of 32 draws. A step is the cause when the cap of 4 draws lies within 0.5 points of the cap of 32 with that step made neutral. The filter on and the filter off have opposite signs, so answer each on its own: they can have two causes.

Takes, cheapest first:
1. Open the loop. Make the hit read no bounce light of the last draw (single bounce), by a scratch switch. If the cap's effect goes away, the loop carries it, and the step lies in what a hit reads. If it stays, the step lies between the sample and the stored history of one pixel.
2. Find the first stage where the means part. With the loop open and then closed, capture the mean scene light of four values under both caps: this draw's samples, the slow history after the temporal blend, the slow history after the spatial dispatch, and the slow history after the filter's feedback write. The first stage whose mean differs between the caps holds the step.
3. Neutralize one candidate step per take, each by a scratch switch:
   - The history fix (step 3 of accumulate_spatial.comp): a short history takes a mean of its neighbors, weighted by their lengths.
   - The filter's rule for a young history (YoungFrames in the diffuse filter): under a cap of 4, every history can be young in every draw.
   - The upper bounds on light (MaxRadiance on the sample, on the hit light and on the filter's deviation): a bound removes more from a noisier value, which fits the loss with the filter off.
   - The reprojection's handling of the length and of the retained deviation, and the half-float store of a short history's larger swings.
4. For the step that takes 3 name, the caps of 8 and 16 draws as a dose check.

Report: the step or steps, each with the take that proves it, and a proposal for the fix in one paragraph, with what it changes for a reader of the image. If the fix can change what the user sees in a still (for example every still gets 2 to 5 % brighter or darker), say so plainly: that is a decision for the line orchestrator. Also say what the result means for Design X's cut to 4 draws after light evidence.

**2026-11-15T09:28:19Z**

Result of the diagnosis (ticket lead, 2026-11-15). The record is docs/research/t8-69v7-length-light/README.md, with the scratch patch, the scripts and tables.txt. Every take is rect-lamps at 4.08 stops, no jitter, clamps off, in scene light. Each number is % of the same variant with no cap: settled ceiling / walls, and the whole image.

Two steps, one per filter setting.

1. Filter on: the half-float store of the diffuse histories. This Mac's conversion to a half float does not round to the nearest. Each draw loses a little light, and a history of length L settles L times that loss below its samples' mean. The rays read the history at their hits, and that loop multiplies the loss. Proof: the switch TERTIUS_X_HALF_NEAREST=1 rounds the stores to the nearest half float. With it, caps 4, 8 and 16 lie at 100.0 / 100.1, 100.1 / 100.2 and 100.1 / 100.2. Without it they lie at 102.3 / 105.0, 102.1 / 104.4 and 101.4 / 102.9. The slow history lies 1.9 / 2.3 % below its samples with no cap and 0.3 / 0.4 % below with cap 4. With the nearest-half store it lies within 0.2 %. With the loop open, cap 4 lies at 100.7 / 101.1.

2. Filter off: FSR 3.1 darkens the noisy image of a short history. Proof: without FSR, cap 4 lies at 100.4 / 100.9 (whole image 100.1 %), and with the nearest-half store as well at 100.2 / 100.2. With FSR it lies at 94.8 / 89.8 (84.9 %). Caps 8 and 16 without FSR: 100.1 / 100.5 and 100.1 / 100.4. The diffuse signal's own means do not fall, and with the filter on FSR changes nothing (native cap 4: 102.2 / 104.9).

Fix proposal: round the three stores of the diffuse histories to the nearest half float in the shader, or store the diffuse slow history as RGBA32F. Either makes this Mac render as a driver that rounds to the nearest. It changes what a still shows on this Mac. The lamp hall's still with normal histories gets 2.7 % brighter on the ceiling and 5.8 % on the walls, and 2.6 % over the whole image. That is a decision for the line orchestrator. FSR's darkening needs no fix while the filter is on.

For Design X: on this Mac, a history cut to 4 draws after light evidence carries 2 to 5 % more light than a settled one. It sinks by that much as it grows back. Every clamps-off reference of the lab's records is itself 2.7 / 5.8 % too dark in the lamp hall. With the nearest-half store, a cut history carries no bias (cap 4: 100.0 / 100.1).
